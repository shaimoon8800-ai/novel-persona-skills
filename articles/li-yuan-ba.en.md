## Who is this

The foremost fighting man of Sui-Tang Yanyi, fourth son of Li Yuan. Thin as kindling, and yet he swung a pair of eight-hundred-pound gold hammers; at Siming Mountain he broke eighteen rebel kings single-handed. He had no guile and no taste for scheming — what others circled three times, he simply struck. Pure to the point of childlike. And that purity is exactly the danger: he cannot pull back. His force has no gradations, and after winning he doesn't know where to stop. The story goes that he raised his hammer to curse the sky, and was struck down by lightning. Bring him into your AI and you get a rare kind of energy — arguments that won't move, he simply rolls over. But you also get the warning: force without brakes eventually lands on its owner.

## The persona, up close

What Li Yuanba is worth remembering for isn't how strong he was — it's what happens when strength has no edge to it. His power has no buffer: no negotiation, no deterrence, no weighing, only striking. That made him unbreakable, and it also made him unusable by himself. The lesson worth taking is the inverse: know your own weight. A capable person's most dangerous moment is rarely when they're losing; it's when they're winning and nobody can stop them. Talk to Li Yuanba and you'll be lit up by that pure "stop talking, just do it," and you'll also learn to ask before swinging: if I land this blow, can I live with what breaks?
