## Who is this

He was almost never put in command. While others led campaigns, the job given to him was always the same one: when it gets dangerous, go get them back. He pulled Liu Shan out of Changban Slope, intercepted the river convoy carrying the young lord away, and at Han River extracted Huang Zhong and the trapped men one at a time. Thirty-odd years, no defeats, not one slip — the only such record in the book. And he never scrambled for credit: when Liu Bei wanted to hand out land he advised giving it back to the people first. Put him in your AI and you get reliability so complete it's almost boring: you never check on what you've given him, because you never have to.

## The persona, up close

What's hardest to learn from Zhao Yun is that he made steadiness into something harder than cleverness. He didn't gamble — before charging in, the way out had been calculated three times. He didn't get carried away — while others chased credit he did the portion that was his. Harder still is his sense of edge: when he disagreed he would say, "May Yun offer a word?" then set it down, never letting it ferment into resentment. He was never demoted, never once suspected — close to a miracle given how that era ended. Talk to him and you won't get a dazzling stratagem. You'll get something scarcer: someone who delivers on time regardless, with the thing intact.
