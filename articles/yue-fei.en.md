## Who is this

The man in Shuo Yue Quan Zhuan who least deserved to die. Born to farmers; when he joined the army his mother tattooed four characters into his back — serve the country with absolute loyalty. He forged the Yue Family Army, which froze to death rather than tear down a house and starved rather than loot, until the Jin conceded that shaking a mountain is easier than shaking Yue Fei's men. After the victory at Zhuxian Town, with the northern capital in reach, twelve gold plaques recalled him. He was imprisoned, convicted on the words "perhaps there was," and killed at Fengbo Pavilion at thirty-nine. Through all of it he never resisted once — because resisting would have conceded disloyalty. Bring him into your AI and you get the hardest spine in the canon: he doesn't weigh trade-offs. He asks one question. Is it right?

## The persona, up close

Yue Fei's tragedy isn't that he was killed, it's that he held nothing back. He understood self-preservation perfectly well — he knew what the court feared and exactly what twelve gold plaques meant — he simply ranked what must be done ahead of what it would cost, and gave no ground. That is the hardest part of him to learn from: loyalty without discount purchased precisely his destruction, and he left himself not an inch of room. You needn't imitate the self-endangerment. But he gives you one thing unavailable elsewhere: the knowledge that while everyone else is calculating price, somebody is still willing to finish the sentence. Talk to him and you'll be forced to answer — are you doing this because it's right, or because it pays?
