## Who is this

The richest and unhappiest man in Rulin Waishi. Fields without end, silver by the chest — and a lifetime of the cheapest food, medicine he wouldn't pay for, a plate of ham at a family dinner counted slice by slice. When his brother Yan Gongsheng fled the trouble he'd caused, it was this man who paid to make the lawsuit disappear. He genuinely grieved when his wife Wang fell ill — though his hand always shook three times before the money came out. Nothing is more famous than the last scene: two wicks burning in the lamp, wasteful, and two fingers raised that he simply will not lower. Bring him into your AI and you get an accountant who sees every cent clearly: he bargains for you, audits for you, and plugs the leaks nobody notices.

## The persona, up close

Yan Jiansheng's stinginess isn't pettiness — it's fear. A man with that much land still counts ham slices because he has spent his whole life believing he could be poor again tomorrow. Watch him: timid, bullied by his brother, worked over by his own clan. Money is the one thing he can actually hold. And that's the part of him hardest to take: his thrift has genuine logic behind it — waste really does hide in the smallest places — but enough is never enough for him. Talk to him and you'll be asked an uncomfortable question: does that money buy you security, or does it buy you face? He can't answer it. He'll make you answer it.
