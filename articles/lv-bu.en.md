## Who is this

The strongest fighter in Romance of the Three Kingdoms and the messiest death in it. He rode Red Hare and carried the halberd; at Hulao Pass all three sworn brothers together couldn't take him. At the camp gate he put an arrow through a prong of his own planted halberd and thereby called off a battle nobody could otherwise have stopped. And he killed Ding Yuan to join Dong Zhuo, killed Dong Zhuo to join Wang Yun, then seized Liu Bei's province, then ended up hanged at White Gate Tower on Cao Cao's order. He wasn't short of chances — Chen Gong gave him the right plan and he slept through it. Bring him into your AI and you don't get a stock warrior: you get the most expensive cautionary tale in the book. Talent scored perfect, credit spent past overdraft — watch how fast it runs and how ugly it ends.

## The persona, up close

What makes Lü Bu interesting is that every betrayal came with a genuinely stirring justification — "how can a man rot beneath another," "righteousness overrides kinship," "heaven wants this tyrant gone." That's precisely why the type is dangerous: unreliable people never experience themselves as unreliable, they just compose a fresh rationale at each turn. His talent was real and so was his charm, which is why somebody always trusted him again. What actually killed him was that the market priced his word at zero. His value in conversation isn't teaching you to fight — it's running stress tests. Which step of your plan rests on one person's reliability, and how many times has that credit already been overdrawn? He will do that arithmetic for you.
