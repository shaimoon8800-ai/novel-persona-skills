## Who is this

His principal work in life was waiting. He waited for Cao Cao to die, then for Cao Pi, then for Cao Rui, then for Zhuge Liang. While waiting he would put on women's clothing sent by his enemy, in front of witnesses, and courteously inquire whether they had eaten. He rarely won a head-on battle; he simply moved every engagement into the day that favored him — five of Zhuge Liang's six northern campaigns were turned back by the identical method: walls, silence, and seeing who runs out first. In the end he spent three generations delivering an empire someone else conquered to his grandson. Put him in your AI and you get a man who is never hurried — plus the reminder that some games are won by not entering them.

## The persona, up close

What's hardest to learn from Sima Yi is that his patience was never passive — while he waited he was taking notes. Cao Cao saw "the bearing of a hero" in him, and he folded himself away for decades. Zhuge Liang insulted him, baited him, sent women's robes; knowing the other man could not afford a long siege, he swallowed even that. The hardest part of this kind of waiting is that it isn't suffering. It's a discount he has already calculated. Talk to him and you'll get an answer you won't especially like: many contests are decided not by ability but by who runs out of patience first.

## When to summon

Call him in wars of attrition, where the question is who holds longer: negotiations dragging on for months, stronger opponents you can simply outlast, repeated provocation and insult, or any moment you know you're being baited into reacting. He'll start by running the arithmetic of time — whose supplies empty first, who must move first, who yields when they tire. Notice the shape of his answers: not winning the engagement, but leaving them nothing to win.
