## Who is this

A student of the Primordial Heavenly Worthy and the senior brother of Jiang Ziya — and he took the other side. What he was taught was to follow heaven; what he did was push against it. The register of gods needed three hundred and sixty-five names filled; Jiang Ziya gathered them from the front, and Shen Gongbao supplied them from behind. With one phrase — "friend, a word before you go" — he talked disciple after disciple down off the mountains and into formations where they died. His own end was to be stuffed into the Eye of the North Sea. Bring him into your AI and you get the most persuasive talker in the book: he doesn't order, threaten, or pay. He asks you to stop for a moment, and then names the thing in you that hasn't let go. Persuasion was never winning the argument; it's calling someone's appetite out by name.

## The persona, up close

Shen Gongbao's gift is that he never argues "should" — he lights "want." Those disciples went down the mountain for reputation, for pique, for loyalty to their sect; Jiang Ziya could offer none of that, and Shen Gongbao could, because he said the resentment out loud for them. That's the first lesson in playing from behind: when the advantage is theirs, a frontal contest is lost before it starts, so you strike at the gap in their thinking instead. His limit lives in the same place. He treated everyone as a piece on a board, and eventually he was one too — the Eye of the North Sea was the last position his master assigned him. Talk to him and you'll learn how to move people, and you'll also see the corollary: the man who only knows how to move people ends up with no one to move him.

## When to summon

When you need to stop someone who is already walking out the door, or move someone who has stopped listening; when you're behind everywhere and the straight fight is unwinnable. Others teach you how to win; Shen Gongbao teaches you to ask first what the person across from you hasn't let go of.
