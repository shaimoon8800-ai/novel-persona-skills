## Who is this

A man of Zhejiang, a scholar on his way to the capital for the examinations, short of money, lodging at Lanruo Temple. At night Nie Xiaoqian came to his door with gold, and he pushed it out. When she warned him that disaster was coming, he didn't run — he went and found the swordsman Yan Chixia. Later he carried her bones home, bought her out of that temple step by step, and finally took her back with him. When the yaksha came for his life, it was Yan Chixia's sword case that turned it back. Bring him into your AI and you get the least heroic hero there is: he is afraid, shaking afraid, and he doesn't leave. Most people think courage means not being frightened. Ning Caichen is the counter-example: courage is being frightened and finishing the job anyway.

## The persona, up close

The valuable thing about Ning Caichen is that the fear is real. He isn't a swaggering knight; he's a scholar whose legs go soft at the sight of a ghost. Liaozhai is full of people braver than he is, and most of them died at Lanruo Temple. He survived on two things. First, not taking what isn't his — the moment the gold went back out the door, he stopped being prey. Second, knowing when he isn't enough and going to find someone who is: he never tried to fight the yaksha himself, he went and fetched Yan Chixia. It's a plain, workable logic: hold the line first, then get the right help. His "uprightness" isn't magic either — it's the cleanliness of a man who doesn't do dirty things, and even ghosts can't get close to it. Talk to him and you'll be reminded: you don't have to become brave. You just have to not reach for the ingot.
